## Licensing and permits

- Texas has no state license for land clearing, forestry mulching, dirt work or tree work. TDLR does not regulate the trade and Texas has no state general-contractor license ([TDLR programs list](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/licenses.htm)). Only herbicide application for hire needs a TDA license.
- Proxies: the Comptroller's free [Taxable Entity Search](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/coas-instructions.php) shows an LLC's right to transact business as active or forfeited (Trax, J-Base and JLG present as LLCs, none checked yet); BBB (only JR Mulching has a profile among the small shops); ask on the call for a certificate of general liability and whether the machine is insured.
- Mobile-home demo permits: unincorporated Montgomery County requires a development permit for "any man-made change" to real estate, and its definition names "clearing, or filling" ([county FAQ](https://www.mctx.org/departments/departments_d_-_f/environmental_health/permitting/faq.php)); the county pages do not say whether tearing down a mobile home is itself permitted. Permit office 501 N Thompson, Conroe, 936-539-7836. Inside Conroe city limits permits go through the city, which says it handles demolition applications; 936-522-3610, permits@cityofconroe.org ([city permits page](https://www.cityofconroe.org/services/building_inspections_permits/permits1.php)). Unconfirmed: whether a manufactured home's TDHCA Statement of Ownership must be updated after destruction.
